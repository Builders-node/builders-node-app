1.1.3Education

Education is one of the things that matters most to us, so we run a few recurring formats to help members sharpen their thinking and build their businesses faster.

  • Workshops (once a week). The best way to pick up a new hard skill. Topics change - economics, planning, AI agents, coding and more.
  • Founder sessions (twice a month). Small, informal sessions with experienced CEOs. Ask them anything: scaling, sales, raising money.
  • Member lectures (on demand). Everyone here knows something the others don't. Share your expertise with the group - it's one of the best ways to learn, and to get known.

On top of that, we run bigger one-off events - hackathons, pitch days and more - planned in advance.

1.1.4Gym

You'll have access to two gyms - one right in Duna Tower, where we live, and a second in St. John's Bay. Both have everything you need: treadmills and other cardio machines, dumbbells, machines for every muscle group, pull-up bars, mats, jump ropes and exercise balls. Open 24/7.

1.1.5Pool

The pool is up on the 8th floor, looking out over the jungle and the ocean - the best place to end a long day, or to start one.

1.1.6Sauna

Up on the 8th floor next to the pool, with the ocean in front of you. Hard to find a better way to reset.

What is Próspera

2.1Definition and how much money startups raised in Próspera

Próspera is a startup city on Roatán - a special economic zone with its own rules, built to make it fast and simple to live, work and start a company. You can register a business in minutes, and there are only four transparent taxes. It's one of the most ambitious experiments in private governance anywhere in the world, and it's working: startups here have raised over $50 million.

03

The vibes

3.1The power of being surrounded by like-minded people

“As iron sharpens iron, so one person sharpens another.”
- Proverbs 27:17

It's about who you become when you live around people who want more from life. Ambition is contagious. When the guy next to you is up at six to run before work, you start running. When someone at dinner is learning to free-dive, or finally shipped the thing they'd been scared to launch, or just seems genuinely happy with how they're living, it quietly raises your own bar. You don't get lectured into it. You just see what's possible, up close, every day - in work, in sport, in how people take care of themselves and each other. Most people leave here not only with more done, but living a little bigger than when they arrived.

5.3Europe

There are no nonstops from Europe to Roatán. You have two routes. The first is through the US: fly to Miami, Houston, Dallas or Atlanta and connect, which takes the least total travel time but requires an ESTA even if you're only transiting. The second avoids the US by going through Spain: Air Europa flies Madrid-San Pedro Sula nonstop about three times a week, around 11 hours. From there, CM Airlines and Aerolíneas Sosa fly San Pedro Sula-Roatán daily, about 45 minutes. To get to Madrid, budget airlines like Ryanair and Wizz Air fly there cheaply from most European cities.

5.5Asia

Connect through Houston. It's the only US hub with both Asia nonstops (Tokyo Narita/Haneda, Taipei) and a daily United flight to Roatán, so most of Asia can reach the island with one stop. From India and the Middle East, Emirates or Qatar to Houston works the same way. Book everything on one ticket and check US transit visa or ESTA rules for your passport. To avoid the US entirely, fly through Madrid: take Air Europa to San Pedro Sula, then a short domestic hop to Roatán.

06

Roatán

6.1Location

Roatán is the largest of Honduras's Bay Islands, set in the western Caribbean on the world's second-largest barrier reef, about a five-hour flight from Toronto.

Builders Node is in the heart of the island, in a quiet area surrounded by a restaurant, beach and jungle - and just a 10-minute drive from the mall and supermarket.

6.2Safety

Roatán isn't mainland Honduras. The Bay Islands are geographically separate and have lower crime rates even compared with other Caribbean islands. Day to day it feels safe: people go out to restaurants, beach bars and parties, walk around public places and enjoy the island like anywhere else. Duna Tower has 24/7 security, gated entry and cameras in common areas.

6.3Weather

Warm all year:

Jan-Mar25°C
Apr-Jun27°C
Jul-Sep28°C
Oct-Dec26°C

October to mid-December is the wetter season, but rain usually comes in short bursts, often at night.

6.4Getting around

Próspera runs a shuttle between Duna Tower and Pristine Bay, and there's almost always someone heading somewhere you can tag along with. For everything else, taxis are easy to find, or you can rent a car, scooter or bike for more freedom.

6.5Health

There's a doctor in the building we live in, a GARMA clinic right in Próspera, plus walk-in clinics and private hospitals around the island. Drink bottled or filtered water, and bring travel insurance with medical evacuation cover.

6.6Language and culture

English is widely spoken on Roatán, since many islanders grew up speaking it at home. Spanish is common too, especially with people from the mainland, so a few basic phrases go a long way. The island has its own mix of cultures - English-speaking islanders, mainland Hondurans, a Garifuna community and expats from all over.

6.7Time zone

Roatán runs on Central Standard Time (GMT-6) all year, with no daylight saving. That's almost the same as North America: your calls with Toronto, New York, Chicago or Vancouver stay inside a normal workday.
